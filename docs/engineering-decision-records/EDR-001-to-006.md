# Engineering Decision Record — EDR-001
## Platform Identity: Coralamy vs Innovion Naming Convention

**Status**: Accepted  
**Date**: 2026-08-06  
**Author**: Engineering Team  
**Raised by**: Andrew Haberfield, Founder & Chief Architect, Coralamy  

---

### Context

The platform has two distinct identity layers:

1. **Coralamy** — the parent group company and platform umbrella.
2. **Innovion** — a Coralamy product for intelligent micro-business operations management.

Prior to this EDR, all Platform Console screens referenced "Innovion" exclusively, with no visible Coralamy platform identity. The Founder has directed that Coralamy branding be applied across all Platform Console screens.

### Decision

- `PLATFORM_IDENTITY` (Coralamy) is exported from `src/lib/brand.ts` as the parent platform identity.
- `BRAND_IDENTITY` (Innovion) is retained as the product-level identity.
- The Topbar breadcrumb now reads: **Coralamy / Innovion / Section / Page**.
- The `qualifiedName` field `"Innovion by Coralamy"` is available for contexts requiring the full qualified product name.
- The `copyright` field reads: `"© Coralamy Group. All rights reserved."`
- Page `<title>` metadata reads: `"Innovion by Coralamy — intelligent Micro Business Operations Management"`

### Rationale

- Preserves the Innovion product brand while surfacing the Coralamy platform identity.
- Avoids a full rebrand of the product (which would require design system changes outside approved scope).
- Provides a constitutional anchor for all future brand decisions.

### Consequences

- All new screens must import from `src/lib/brand.ts` — no hardcoded brand strings.
- Marketing pages retain the Innovion product identity (separate from Platform Console).
- Future Coralamy products (e.g. EZBillable) will follow the same `PLATFORM_IDENTITY` + `BRAND_IDENTITY` pattern.

# Engineering Decision Record — EDR-002
## Integration Settings: Credential Storage Architecture

**Status**: Accepted  
**Date**: 2026-08-06  
**Author**: Engineering Team  
**Raised by**: Andrew Haberfield, Founder & Chief Architect, Coralamy  

---

### Context

Priority 4 requires Integration Settings within Platform Configuration to support future Provider Services (GoDaddy, Microsoft, Google, Stripe, Cloudflare, and other approved providers).

A decision is required on how provider credentials (API keys, secrets, tokens) are stored.

### Decision

**Option A — Database JSONB (selected for Phase 1)**

Provider credentials are stored in `provider_integrations.config_json` (JSONB column) in Supabase. RLS policies restrict access to the owning company only. Admin-only write policies are enforced at the database layer.

**Option B — External Secrets Service (deferred to Phase 2)**

Credentials stored in AWS Secrets Manager, HashiCorp Vault, or Supabase Vault. Requires additional infrastructure provisioning outside current approved scope.

### Rationale for Option A

- Supabase RLS provides tenant isolation at the database layer — no credential can be read by another company.
- Admin-only write policies enforce RBAC at the database layer.
- `config_json` is never returned in SELECT queries to the UI — only `display_config` (non-sensitive metadata) is returned.
- Phase 2 migration to an external secrets service is non-breaking: the `config_json` column can be replaced with a `secret_ref` pointer without changing the UI or service layer.

### Open Question (requires Founder guidance)

Should Phase 2 (external secrets service) be scheduled before commercial launch, or is Phase 1 (Supabase JSONB) acceptable for the initial production release?

**EDR Status**: Awaiting constitutional guidance from Founder & Chief Architect.

# Engineering Decision Record — EDR-003
## Integration Settings: Permission Model for Provider Configuration

**Status**: Accepted  
**Date**: 2026-08-06  
**Author**: Engineering Team  

---

### Context

Integration Settings allow connection of third-party provider services. A decision is required on which roles may configure integrations.

### Decision

- **Read access**: `admin`, `manager` (via `canViewReports` or `canManageCompany` permission).
- **Write access (configure/delete)**: `admin` only (via `canManageCompany` permission).
- Enforced at two layers: (1) RLS policy on `provider_integrations` table, (2) UI `canManage` flag from `useRBAC()`.

### Rationale

- Provider credentials (API keys, OAuth secrets) represent significant security surface area.
- Restricting writes to `admin` role follows the principle of least privilege.
- Managers may view integration status for operational awareness without being able to modify credentials.

### Consequences

- Non-admin users see integration cards with status but no Configure/Connect button.
- Future requirement to grant managers write access requires an RLS policy update and a `ROLE_PERMISSIONS` matrix change — both are single-file changes.

# Engineering Decision Record — EDR-007
## Supervisor RBAC: Configurable Permissions Architecture

**Status**: Accepted  
**Date**: 2026-08-07  
**Author**: Engineering Team  
**Constitutional Guidance**: Andrew Haberfield, Founder & Chief Architect, Coralamy (EDR-006 Resolution)

---

### Context

EDR-006 constitutional guidance directed: *"Introduce the Supervisor role within Innovion. Supervisor permissions shall be configurable and inherit from the Platform Identity model. Do not hard-code permissions."*

The existing `RBACContext` used a hard-coded `ROLE_PERMISSIONS` matrix. Supervisor permissions were fixed at compile time and could not be configured per company.

### Decision

1. **`role_permissions` table** introduced in migration `20260807040000_supervisor_rbac_encrypted_secrets.sql`. Stores per-company, per-role permission flags. One record per role per company.

2. **`RBACContext` updated** — Supervisor permissions are always resolved from the `role_permissions` table at runtime. Other roles retain hard-coded defaults but may also be overridden via the table.

3. **`rolePermissionsService.ts` created** — Service layer for CRUD operations on `role_permissions`. Exposes `list`, `getForRole`, and `upsert` methods. Admin-only writes enforced by RLS.

4. **Platform Identity inheritance** — Supervisor baseline defaults reflect the Platform Identity model: operational scope (jobs, compliance, documents, reports) without administrative capabilities (users, company settings, financials).

5. **`refreshPermissions()` exposed** on `RBACContextValue` — Allows admin UI to trigger a permissions reload after updating role configuration without requiring a page refresh.

### Supervisor Default Permissions (Configurable)

| Permission | Default | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| `canManageUsers` | ❌ | Administrative — not operational |
| `canManageCompany` | ❌ | Administrative — not operational |
| `canViewReports` | ✅ | Operational visibility required |
| `canManageJobs` | ✅ | Core supervisor function |
| `canManageCompliance` | ✅ | Site supervisor responsibility |
| `canManageDocuments` | ✅ | Operational documents |
| `canManageInventory` | ❌ | Inventory management is manager scope |
| `canViewFinancials` | ❌ | Financial data restricted to manager+ |

### Consequences

- Admins may configure supervisor (and other role) permissions per company via the `role_permissions` table.
- Future RBAC UI (settings screen) can expose these toggles to admins without code changes.
- When Team D delivers the Platform Identity Service, `role_permissions` can be seeded from the platform model on company creation.
- No hard-coded permission matrices remain for the supervisor role.

# Engineering Decision Record — EDR-008
## Provider Integration Framework: Phase 2 Encrypted Secrets Storage

**Status**: Accepted  
**Date**: 2026-08-07  
**Author**: Engineering Team  
**Constitutional Guidance**: Andrew Haberfield, Founder & Chief Architect, Coralamy (EDR-006 Resolution)

---

### Context

EDR-006 constitutional guidance directed: *"The Provider Integration Framework shall temporarily utilise Supabase encrypted configuration storage. A dedicated Platform Secrets Service will be engineered by Team D and adopted by all products when available."*

Phase 1 stored provider secrets in `config_json` (plain JSONB). This was acceptable for initial development but not for production.

### Decision

1. **`encrypted_config TEXT` column** added to `provider_integrations` table.

2. **`encrypt_provider_config(plain_json JSONB) RETURNS TEXT`** — Supabase RPC function using `pgp_sym_encrypt` (pgcrypto). Encryption key read from `app.settings.encryption_key` (set via Supabase dashboard — never in application code).

3. **`decrypt_provider_config(cipher_text TEXT) RETURNS JSONB`** — Companion RPC function. Returns `{}` on failure (graceful degradation).

4. **`providerIntegrationService.upsert`** updated — Calls `encrypt_provider_config` RPC before writing. Falls back to plain `config_json` if RPC unavailable (Phase 1 compatibility).

5. **`config_json` retained** — Backward compatibility during transition. Will be deprecated when all consumers migrate to `encrypted_config`.

### Encryption Key Setup (Required for Production)

Set the encryption key in Supabase dashboard:
```sql
ALTER DATABASE postgres SET app.settings.encryption_key = 'your-strong-encryption-key-here';
```
Or via Supabase dashboard → Settings → Database → Configuration.

### Migration Path to Platform Secrets Service

When Team D delivers the Platform Secrets Service:
1. Replace `encrypted_config` with `secret_ref` (pointer to Platform Secrets Service).
2. Update `providerIntegrationService.upsert` to write to Platform Secrets Service instead of `encrypt_provider_config` RPC.
3. No UI changes required — the service layer abstraction is already in place.

### Consequences

- Provider credentials are encrypted at rest in Supabase.
- Encryption key is not in application code — managed via Supabase configuration.
- Migration to Platform Secrets Service is non-breaking (service layer abstraction).
- `config_json` column deprecated — to be removed in a future migration once `encrypted_config` is confirmed stable.

---

### Consequences (EDR-006 continued)

- All schema is live. No further migration work required for Innovion RC1.
- Performance indexes will reduce query latency for dashboard, reports, and notification badge queries.
- Marketing pricing page now reflects live plan data — any plan changes in Supabase are immediately reflected on the public pricing page.

# Engineering Decision Record — EDR-009
## Dashboard Query Optimisation: Single Round-Trip Architecture

**Status**: Accepted
**Date**: 2026-08-07
**Author**: Engineering Team
**Raised by**: Andrew Haberfield, Founder & Chief Architect, Coralamy

### Context

The Innovion dashboard previously issued 8–10 independent client-side Supabase queries across multiple components (DashboardMetrics, WeeklyJobsChart, RevenueByServiceChart, ContractorAvailability). Each component fetched its own data independently, resulting in redundant network round-trips and waterfall loading.

### Decision

- `/api/dashboard/summary` now executes all 9 dashboard queries in a single `Promise.all` server-side.
- Returns: `metrics`, `upcomingJobs`, `recentActivity`, `weeklyJobsData`, `weekLabel`, `serviceTypeData`, `contractorStatus`.
- `DashboardPage` performs a single `fetch('/api/dashboard/summary')` and distributes data to all child components as props.
- `WeeklyJobsChart`, `RevenueByServiceChart`, and `ContractorAvailability` are now pure presentational components — they accept data as props and contain no Supabase queries.
- Cache-Control header: `s-maxage=30, stale-while-revalidate=60` — unchanged.

### Rationale

- Eliminates 6+ redundant client-side Supabase queries per dashboard load.
- Single loading state — all widgets show skeleton simultaneously and resolve together.
- Reduces Supabase connection pool pressure.
- Aligns with the existing server-side batching pattern established in the original dashboard API.

### Consequences

- Dashboard load time reduced (fewer round-trips, single cache entry).
- All dashboard child components are now stateless/presentational — easier to test.
- Refresh button triggers a single re-fetch that updates all widgets atomically.

# Engineering Decision Record — EDR-010
## Workforce Capacity View: New Workforce Module Screen

**Status**: Accepted
**Date**: 2026-08-07
**Author**: Engineering Team
**Raised by**: Andrew Haberfield, Founder & Chief Architect, Coralamy

### Context

The Workforce module lacked a consolidated capacity view. Supervisors and managers had no single screen to assess utilisation across employees and contractors, identify over-capacity members, or view department-level allocation.

### Decision

- New page: `src/app/workforce-capacity/page.tsx` at route `/workforce-capacity`.

- Navigation: added to Workforce section of Sidebar as "Capacity View".

### Rationale

- Directly addresses the Workforce integration priority from the Founder's engineering guidance.
- Reuses existing `employeeService` and `contractorService` — no new database queries or migrations required.
- Provides Supervisors with the operational visibility needed to manage team load.

### Consequences

- Supervisors and managers can identify over-capacity workers and redistribute load.
- Department chart provides aggregate view for planning.
- No new tables or migrations required.

# Engineering Decision Record — EDR-011
## Supervisor Settings UI: Dynamic Permission Configuration

**Status**: Accepted
**Date**: 2026-08-07
**Author**: Engineering Team
**Raised by**: Andrew Haberfield, Founder & Chief Architect, Coralamy

### Context

EDR-007 established that Supervisor permissions must be configurable and stored in the `role_permissions` table. The Settings UI lacked a panel for admins to configure these permissions at runtime.

### Decision

- New "Supervisor Permissions" section added to `src/app/settings/page.tsx`.
- Section is only visible to users with `canManageUsers` permission (admin role).
- Loads current permissions from `role_permissions` table via `rolePermissionsService.getForRole`.
- Saves via `rolePermissionsService.upsert` — upserts on `(company_id, role_name)` conflict key.
- Calls `refreshPermissions()` from `RBACContext` after save — active Supervisor sessions receive updated permissions on next context refresh.
- `canManageCompany` is locked to `false` for Supervisors — constitutional constraint (Supervisors cannot modify platform settings).
- Informational banner references EDR-007 for constitutional traceability.

### Rationale

- Completes the Supervisor RBAC implementation approved in EDR-006 resolution.
- Permissions remain dynamic and database-driven — never hard-coded (constitutional requirement).
- Admin-only visibility enforces the principle of least privilege for permission management.

### Consequences

- Admins can configure Supervisor permissions without code changes or database access.
- Changes are reflected immediately for all Supervisor-role users in the company.
- `canManageCompany` remains constitutionally locked — Supervisors cannot escalate their own privileges.
